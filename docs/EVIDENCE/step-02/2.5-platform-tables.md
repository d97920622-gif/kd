## 2. Design decisions

**Prices are `numeric(18,4)` and USD only.** `plans.monthly_price`,
`plans.yearly_price`, `subscriptions.price_amount`,
`coupons.discount_value` and `coupon_redemptions.discount_amount` are all
`numeric(18,4) NOT NULL`. `currency_code` is checked to `'USD'` on every table
that carries it; sub-step 2.6 adds the `currencies` foreign key in its own
migration, because the table does not exist yet. The probe reads a stored
`12.0000` back as the string `12.0000`, not as a float.

**The plan table is the owner of a published price, and the page agrees.**
`lib/i18n/strings/en/pricing.ts` states that "the plan table wins and this file
is edited" and names the four slugs `free`, `starter`, `pro`, `enterprise`.
Sub-step 2.14 seeds exactly those four rows with the published figures
(`0`, `12`/`120`, `29`/`290`), so the page and the database cannot disagree
without one of them being edited.

**A subscription stores the price it was sold at.** `price_amount` is a
snapshot, not a read of `plans.monthly_price`. A later plan price change must
not rewrite what a tenant already agreed to; the invoice quotes the snapshot.
That is why the column exists beside `plan_id` rather than being derived.

**One live subscription per tenant.** The partial unique index on
`(company_id) WHERE status IN ('trialing','active','past_due','paused')` means
an upgrade is "cancel the old row and insert the new one in one transaction",
which is how the proration of step 4 will record it. Two live rows cannot
exist, so the entitlement materialisation can never read two answers.

**Entitlements are the materialised answer.** One row per tenant per feature
with `is_enabled`, an optional `limit_value` plus `limit_period`, its `source`
(`plan`, `trial`, `coupon`, `override`) and an optional expiry. The guard reads
this table; the engine that writes it is step 4. `chk_entitlements_disabled_
has_no_limit` removes the one ambiguity a reader could hit (a disabled feature
that still carries a cap).

**A feature flag is not an entitlement.** `feature_flags` gates a release
(global row, optional per-tenant override, rollout percentage); `entitlements`
bills a feature. Two tables, two owners, and the migration says so in both
directions. The CEO toggle of FEATURE_LIST 3616 needs super_admin approval in
the flow, not in this table.

**Coupons are platform rows with a one-time-per-tenant policy.** The policy is
the partial unique index on `coupon_redemptions (coupon_id, company_id)`, not a
column. See section 5 for why that replaced a `max_redemptions_per_company`
column.

## 5. The coupon policy defect and its fix

The first draft of `coupons` carried both
`max_redemptions_per_company integer` and a partial unique index on
`coupon_redemptions (coupon_id, company_id)`. The probe found the
contradiction immediately: after a redemption was reversed, the tenant could
not redeem again (unique violation), and a coupon whose column said "3 per
tenant" could never reach 2.

Two coherent designs exist. The chosen one is **one use per tenant, enforced by
the index**, because FEATURE_LIST 723 names the one-time policy as the rule and
a column that can disagree with an index is a second owner. The
`max_redemptions_per_company` column was removed, the migration header records
that a future multi-use coupon needs a new migration that drops the index and
adds the counter, and `max_redemptions_total` remains for the global cap.

A reversed redemption still counts as used. Reversing a redemption is an
accounting correction (a refund), not a fresh entitlement, and the probe pins
that behaviour.

The same run also caught two malformed probe statements of mine (three columns
with two values) that reported `42601` and never tested uniqueness; the
corrected statements report `23505`.

  keys to `currencies` and `countries` in 2.6, in that sub-step's migration.
